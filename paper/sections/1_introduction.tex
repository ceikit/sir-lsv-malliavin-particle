\section{Introduction}\label{sec: introduction}
While for short-dated options (less than 1 year), assuming constant interest rates does not lead to
significant mispricing, for long-dated options the effect of interest rate volatility becomes increasingly
pronounced with increasing maturity and can become as important as that of the FX spot volatility.

We consider the three-factor model in which the FX spot has a local volatility $\sigma(t,S)$ and the domestic and foreign short rates follow Hull-White processes. The local volatility is the unknown: it has to be chosen so that the model reprices the market smile. With deterministic rates the answer is Dupire's formula, $\sigma=\sigma_{Dup}$. With stochastic rates the local variance is the Dupire variance minus a correction (Proposition \ref{prop: calibration}),
\[
\sigma(t,K)^2 = \sigma_{Dup}(t,K)^2 - \frac{ \mathbb{E}^d\big[ D_{0t}^d\big(K(r_t^d-r^0_d)-S_t(r_t^f-r^0_f)\big)\mathbb{I}_{S_t>K} \big]}{ \frac{1}{2}K^2\partial^2_KC(t,K) },
\]
where $D^d_{0t}$ is the domestic discount factor, $r^0_d$ and $r^0_f$ are the forward rates, $C$ is the market call price, and the expectation is taken in the model. The local volatility therefore depends on the joint law of the spot and the rates which it generates itself: the calibrated model is a nonlinear diffusion in the sense of McKean.

The particle method of Guyon and Henry-Labord\`ere \cite{guyonLabordere} solves equations of this kind by simulation. A system of $N$ paths is advanced in time; at each time step the expectation is replaced by an average over the paths, which gives the local volatility used on the next step. Applied to the formula above, the estimator has two weak points, both in the wings of the smile: the numerator is the expectation of a tail event, and the denominator is the market density $\partial^2_KC$. For one stochastic rate, \cite{guyonLabordere} remove both by a Malliavin integration by parts: the correction becomes a conditional expectation given $S_t=K$, estimated by kernel regression, of quantities which under Hull-White dynamics can be simulated along each path.

\paragraph{The difficulty with two rates.} Under the domestic measure the foreign rate is not autonomous: its drift contains the local volatility. The Malliavin derivatives of the spot and of the foreign rate then solve a coupled linear system indexed by two times, with random coefficients, which has no closed form (Section \ref{subsubsec: obstacle}).

\paragraph{Results.} The calibration condition is first written under the domestic measure only, as above, so that one system of particles suffices. The main result (Proposition \ref{prop: main}) is that, for Hull-White rates,
\[
\sigma(t,K)^2=\sigma_{Dup}(t,K)^2-2\,\mathbb{E}^{d^t}\big[\ell^d_t-\ell^f_t\,\big|\,S_t=K\big],
\]
where $\ell^d$ and $\ell^f$ are two of four scalar processes carried by each path and solving the forward equations (\ref{eq: sensitivities}). They are obtained by integrating the Malliavin derivatives against the Hull-White kernels instead of solving for them. The formula contains neither $\partial^2_KC$ nor a tail expectation. It is an equivalence: a model which exists and is regular enough is calibrated if and only if its local volatility satisfies it.

Around this result, Section \ref{sec: theory} collects what can be said on existence (an exact solution for flat smiles, a necessary condition on the smile, well-posedness of a regularised equation) and gives a deterministic formulation: for a prescribed volatility, the discounted density and the first moments of the four processes solve a closed system of five linear forward equations. Section \ref{sec: evidence} gives the numerical evidence. The identities behind the formula hold by Monte Carlo within one standard error. In a calibration to CME EUR/USD option settlements, the repricing error due to the stochastic rates is at most 0.005 volatility points over the quoted expiries with the correction, against 0.19 and 0.47 without it for two sets of rate parameters. In a synthetic test, two PDE solvers and the particle method recover the same hidden volatility to within 0.003 volatility points.

\paragraph{Related work.} The use of Malliavin integration by parts to remove the density from the stochastic-rates correction is not new. For one stochastic rate it appears in Benhamou, Gruz and Rivoira \cite{benhamouGruzRivoira}, who obtain the correction as a conditional expectation of an integral of the first variation process of the spot, and, in exact form and together with the particle method, in \cite{guyonLabordere}. In \cite{benhamouGruzRivoira} the Malliavin derivative of the log-spot in the direction of the rate driver is taken to be the correlation times its derivative in the direction of the spot driver, so that the correction vanishes at zero correlation, which it does not in the model considered here (check (b) of Section \ref{subsec: checks}). The conditional expectation is then approximated by evaluating the volatility at the strike, and the local volatility is computed by a fixed-point iteration. Bloch and Nakashima \cite{blochNakashima} treat stochastic domestic and foreign rates. They match the variance of the logarithm of the spot with and without stochastic rates, display a correction of the same domestic-minus-foreign form as (\ref{eq: mainFormula}), written with conditional covariances, and compute it for a locally constant drift with the volatility evaluated at the strike, by the same fixed-point iteration or by solving a quadratic equation at each maturity; their examples set the foreign rate to zero. We found in neither paper an equivalent of the four forward equations (\ref{eq: sensitivities}), in particular of the terms produced by the foreign change of measure. What this paper may add is therefore narrow: an exact finite-dimensional realisation of the correction for the coupled Gaussian-rates model, and the calibration equivalence of Proposition \ref{prop: main}. No claim of priority against the whole literature is made.

Monte Carlo calibration of local volatility in an FX setting with two stochastic rates is studied in \cite{ogetbilGanesanHientzsch} (see also \cite{ogetbilHientzsch} for the corresponding Dupire formula), and a particle method with control variates for a four-factor FX local-stochastic volatility model in \cite{cozma}; an optimal transport approach to stochastic-rates calibration is developed in \cite{josephLoeperObloj}. Existence of calibrated models has been studied for local-stochastic volatility with deterministic rates \cite{abergelTachet,jourdainZhou,lackerShkolnikovZhang,djete,mustapha}; those results do not cover the present equation.

\paragraph{What is not done.} Existence and uniqueness of the calibrated model for a general smile, and convergence of the particle method, are open. The representation uses Gaussian rates with deterministic volatility, and the spot has no stochastic volatility. No comparison has been made with control-variate particle methods such as \cite{cozma}. Section \ref{sec: limits} discusses these limits.
